## LAYER 1 — TEXT (lesson / video script)

Let me guess how your last chat with AI went. You typed "write me a post about my product," hit enter, and got back… something. Smooth, polished, and so generic it could belong to anyone selling anything. You closed the tab and decided AI is overrated.

Or maybe you're the opposite — you've gotten good at this. You write the long, careful prompts, the big multi-part ones that actually work. But they live scattered across your Notes app, an old Google Doc, a screenshot somewhere — and the one you need is never where you left it. So every new chat, you're either re-explaining your whole business from scratch, or digging for that perfect prompt you wrote three weeks ago.

Beginner or pro, it's the same tax: you keep paying for context with your time. Sound familiar?

Here's what actually happened. You didn't hire a bad employee. You hired a brilliant one — and told him nothing about the job.

Picture it. A world-class assistant shows up day one. Knows marketing, writing, design — all of it. You say "write a post," and walk out of the room. He's never seen your product. Doesn't know your customer. Has no idea how you talk. So he writes something safe and average. Of course he does. It's the only thing he can do with nothing to go on.

The difference between a useless answer and a brilliant one isn't the model. It's the context. Context is everything you know about your business that lives in your head and never makes it into the chat — who your customer is, what your product actually does, how you sound, what you'd never say.

The AI isn't a mind reader. It's a mirror. Feed it nothing, it reflects nothing. Feed it your world, and it reflects your world back — sharper than you'd have written it yourself.

Here's the rule that changes everything:
**A model is only as good as the context you give it.**

Same AI. Same question. Two completely different worlds, depending on what you load in first. Type "write a post" cold, you get vanilla. Spend two minutes first — here's my product, here's my customer, here's my tone, here are three posts I love — and the next answer doesn't read like AI. It reads like you on your best day.

And this isn't a Claude trick. The same rule runs ChatGPT, Gemini, any of them. We build it here on Claude because today it's the easiest place to make it stick — but the method doesn't care which model you use.

**So why does everyone skip this?** Because typing all that context, every single time, is exhausting. By the third chat, you just… don't.

That's the whole problem this module solves. You don't re-explain your business to a human employee every morning — they already know it. Same with AI. We load your world in once, save it, and from then on your team just knows. Who you are. What you sell. How you sound. That saved context is the entire difference between a vending machine and a real employee.

**So how do you load a whole business without it turning into a mess?** You sort it into two folders. Not ten. Two.

**Folder one is you** — your AI Clone: how you think, how you sound, the things you'd never say.
**Folder two is your work** — your Business: your customer, your product, your offer, your competitors.

We always go the same direction — big picture first, details after. One folder for who you are, one for what you do. That's the whole map of this module. Every lesson from here just fills those two folders, one file at a time.

And you're not starting from a blank page. The AI employees in this course aren't theory — they already exist, and they already work. We run them ourselves every day: one that builds scroll-stopping carousels, one that reads a target market cold and tells you who you're really selling to. The same ones that have pulled in real clients and hundreds of thousands of views. You get them as ready-made files you drop straight into your AI — Skills you'll get hands-on with in a later lesson. You start from proven, not from zero.

So before the next lesson, plant the seed. Open your AI — Claude, or whatever you've got — and type three bullets, right into the chat:
One — what you sell, in one sentence.
Two — who buys it.
Three — one phrase you say all the time that sounds like you.

Three bullets. Thirty seconds. Don't polish them.

That's the seed of your two folders — the product and the customer go to Business, the phrase that sounds like you goes to AI Clone. Next lesson we open folder one, your AI Clone, and turn those three lines into something your whole team reads before every single task.

Let's go build your second brain.
